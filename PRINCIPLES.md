# Principles

## Purpose

Advance research by exploring ideas quickly and producing results others can check and build on. Keep shared concepts and routine choices consistent so contributions fit together and the project remains maintainable.

## Contributions

<a id="motivation"></a>

### Motivation: Explain why each change is worth making.

The problem or research question gives others a basis for judging the change's scope, evidence, and tradeoffs.

<a id="coherence"></a>

### Coherence: Organize code and explanations so others can understand and build on them.

Clear responsibilities and well-placed explanations let others build on the work and keep shared concepts consistent as the project grows.

<a id="verification"></a>

### Verification: Support claims with evidence others can check.

Research results are useful only when others can check how they were obtained and where they may fail.

<a id="provenance"></a>

### Provenance: Keep contributions traceable to their sources.

Recording origins and adaptations lets others evaluate and build on inherited work, while preserving credit for its contributors.

## Working together

<a id="proportionality"></a>

### Proportionality: Match review and integration work to a change's risk and reach.

Extra steps should improve clarity, confidence, coordination, or traceability. Otherwise they delay useful research without helping future maintenance.

## Applying the principles

Rules apply these principles to recurring decisions and resolve tradeoffs. They also settle routine choices, so contributors need not renegotiate them. Agree on exceptions to required rules explicitly so shared expectations remain predictable.
